\begin{abstract}
Home IoT devices run unattended, with no one to defend them. We present \textsc{Sentinel}, an open-source appliance that detects
anomalous traffic with a sequence autoencoder over Kitsune features, labels
events with MITRE ATT\&CK techniques using a language model running on the
appliance, and enforces blocks through nftables under a deterministic,
auditable policy, with honeypots feeding the same path. We then evaluate the whole system rigorously. On N-BaIoT, with entire
devices held out of training, the detector catches eight of ten attack
classes almost perfectly yet misses nearly all BASHLITE TCP and UDP floods,
\NblindShare{} of attack windows, for which its scores barely separate attack
from benign traffic; false positives rise from \NlstmFPRin{} to
\NlstmFPRout{} on unseen devices, driven mostly by one device. The blind spot
is shared by a simpler autoencoder, which matches the sequence model at lower
cost. In a pre-registered test the local LLM named a correct ATT\&CK
technique for \NllmAsbTech{} of attack windows, labelling almost everything as
command-and-control even when given a traffic summary, while its high
severities would still have triggered blocks for \NllmAsbBlock{} of attacks.
We also found that \textsc{Sentinel}'s live feature extractor computed 40 of
115 features differently from the training data, and fixed it with a parity
test built from invariants of the training set. Every in-house stage takes
under a millisecond; the path is dominated by the LLM (\NllmLatMed{}\,s) and a
blocking threat-intelligence lookup (median \NotxFullMed{}\,s). We release the
code, the pre-registration and all results.
\end{abstract}
