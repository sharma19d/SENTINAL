Homora checks the home network your phone is on. It does three jobs:

<b>Who's on my Wi-Fi.</b> Finds the devices on the network, names them from what they announce, and lets you mark them as yours. Anything left unmarked deserves a closer look. It also has a "find this device" helper and a guide for getting someone off your Wi-Fi.

<b>Is my network set up safely.</b> Checks Wi-Fi encryption, WPS, UPnP, Telnet, FTP, VNC, open camera streams and an unencrypted router settings page. Every finding comes with a fix.

<b>Where's my Wi-Fi good.</b> A live signal meter with router latency, a sound mode for walking around, saved rooms ranked best to worst, and a helper for smart devices that won't connect on 2.4 GHz.

<b>A scanner, never an attack tool.</b> It only checks the private network your phone is on, with a short list of ports and read-only questions. It never tries passwords, sends exploits or disconnects devices.

<b>Private.</b> Device lists, names and rooms stay on your phone. The F-Droid version has no analytics, no crash reporting and makes no connection to any server of ours.

Android only shows Wi-Fi names and scan results to apps with the location permission. Homora asks for it with an explanation and never reads your location.
